\subsection{Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods on Kaggle Dataset}

To evaluate the clinical efficacy and computational efficiency of the proposed framework, a rigorous comparative benchmarking was conducted against fifteen state-of-the-art architectures evaluated on the multi-class Kaggle CT Kidney Dataset. The comprehensive results across both diagnostic accuracy metrics and operational edge-deployment parameters are summarized in Table~4.

As presented in Table~4, classical machine learning approaches (e.g., Rajendran et al. [25] and Tarul et al. [20]) offer low latency ($<10$\,ms) and high throughput ($>110$\,FPS), yet fall short in classification accuracy ($95.20\%$--$96.50\%$). Conversely, heavyweight deep learning ensembles and Vision Transformers (e.g., Islam et al. [37] and Obaid et al. [18]) achieve high diagnostic accuracies ($>99.30\%$), but at the cost of prohibitive computational overheads (up to $163.91$\,M parameters, $19.62$\,G FLOPs, and latency exceeding $200$\,ms), hindering real-time clinical deployment. 

In contrast, our proposed PCSA-KidneyNeXt architecture delivers an optimal Pareto-efficient trade-off in Table~4. It attains state-of-the-art diagnostic performance with \textbf{99.92\% accuracy}, \textbf{99.82\% precision}, and \textbf{99.93\% recall}, while requiring only \textbf{2.11\,M parameters} and \textbf{0.75\,G FLOPs}. Operating at a rapid latency of \textbf{29.1\,ms} and an operational throughput of \textbf{34.4\,FPS}, the proposed model satisfies all requisite benchmarks for seamless point-of-care radiological integration.
